Understanding Fractions

Fractions are parts of a whole. When we divide something into equal parts, each part is called a fraction. For example, if you cut a pizza into 8 equal slices and eat 3 slices, you have eaten 3/8 of the pizza.

The top number in a fraction is called the numerator. It tells us how many parts we have. The bottom number is called the denominator. It tells us how many equal parts the whole thing was divided into.

Adding Fractions with Same Denominators

When fractions have the same denominator, we can add them easily. We just add the numerators and keep the same denominator. For example: 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4. This is like saying "one quarter plus two quarters equals three quarters."

Comparing Fractions

To compare fractions, we need to look at both the numerator and denominator. If two fractions have the same denominator, the one with the larger numerator is bigger. For example, 3/5 is larger than 2/5. If the denominators are different, we need to find equivalent fractions with the same denominator first.

Real World Examples

Fractions are everywhere in real life! When we cook, we use fractions like 1/2 cup of flour or 3/4 teaspoon of salt. When we tell time, we say "quarter past" (1/4 of an hour) or "half past" (1/2 of an hour). Understanding fractions helps us in many daily activities.
